# Candidates — Six Words, One Story

Judge criteria (most important first): (1) complete story in exactly six words;
(2) a turn, a surprise, or a whole life implied; (3) every word earns its place;
(4) reads cleanly aloud; (5) leaves something unsaid. Cleverness for its own
sake does not count.

## 1. WINNER — "Forty years married. First night apart."
- Words: 6. Characters: 39.
- Why it wins: the first sentence holds a whole life (forty years together);
  the second is the turn — the first night apart, after all that time. The
  cause is never said (death? hospital? estrangement?), so the reader fills it
  in. Every word earns its place: "Forty" sets the scale, "First" sets the
  turn, "apart" lands the loss. Two short beats, clean rhythm aloud. It is a
  complete story — a beginning (forty years), a middle (the marriage), and an
  ending (the night apart) — in six words.

## 2. "The lighthouse keeper lit nothing tonight."
- Words: 6. Characters: 42.
- Strong image and a sharp turn (a keeper whose one job is the light, and
  tonight he lights nothing). But two words are spent on the setup
  ("lighthouse keeper"), and it is one scene rather than a life arc: the
  reader sees a moment, not a whole story with a before and an after. Loses
  to #1 on criterion 2 — less of a life implied.

## 3. "He returned home. Nobody remembered him."
- Words: 6. Characters: 40.
- A real arc: he left, he came back, he was forgotten. The turn is strong.
  But the first sentence is pure setup and the second does all the work; the
  story's emotional weight rests on one beat. It also leaves less unsaid than
  #1 — the meaning is spelled out ("nobody remembered him"), where #1 only
  implies the loss. Loses to #1 on criteria 3 and 5.

## 4. "She finally forgave him. Too late."
- Words: 6. Characters: 34.
- Good turn ("Too late" reframes the forgiveness) and it leaves the cause
  unsaid. But "finally" is doing heavy lifting and the story is thinner: it is
  one act (forgiveness) plus a verdict, with no whole life behind it. #1
  carries forty years in the same six words. Loses to #1 on criterion 2.
